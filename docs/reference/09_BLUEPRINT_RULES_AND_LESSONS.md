## Part A. The rules

### R1. Never present a placeholder or default as data

- **Rule:** a value the source does not show is `""`, `None` or "not available (<why>)". No default number, amount, name, id, university or intake may ever reach a reply, a prompt, a sheet or a published record. Make "demo mode" an explicit, visible choice.
- **Incident:**
  - `index.php` parser (baseline): `dict.get(label, <placeholder>)` with case-sensitive labels. When the portal printed "Total students" instead of "Total Students", the bot reported **262** applicants, **31** registrations this week, **2** under review and **21** documents, all hard-coded, and fed them to the LLM brief. A number `1565` for Hanyang came from the CSS colour `#1565c0`.
  - Verified-students parser: every student without an amount chip got **"20,000.00 BDT"** (209 of 322 stamps had no "Verified income"), inflating revenue; missing names and verifiers became "Student" / "Admin".
  - Students list: positional parsing gave every student the intake "March 2027", invented ids `HNG-SL-<n>` and the university "Pending Allocation".
  - `/alerts` always said "No urgent alerts" because it read an `urgent_alerts` key the live dashboard never had (the REST route `/api/dashboard/alerts` still returns `[]`).
  - `MOCK_MODE` defaults to `True` in `src\config.py`: a `.env` without `MOCK_MODE=false` answers with invented demo data, and the migration's `/stats` acceptance check would have passed on it.
  - The first Supabase dry run found the portal's **own** typed fillers kept as values (R29) and the second found Cloudflare's stand-in e-mail (R28): placeholders need not come from your code.
- **Now:** `parsers.parse_hangeul_live_dashboard` and `_tile_value` return `None` when a tile is missing or ambiguous (`f8fefa4`); `parsers.verification` takes the amount only from `.pf.verified` / `.pf.paid` (`e0d47ab`); `format_verified_students_report` prints `—` for a missing field; `/alerts` shows the live "Needs attention" card (`344a247`); MIGRATION.md's three `.env` checks start with `MOCK_MODE=false`; the Supabase layer publishes nothing in mock mode (`command_hooks.active`, `bot_jobs.hand_over`, `full_picture.run`).
- **Pin it:** `tests\test_brief.py` "a missing tile is None, never a placeholder", "the verified reader never makes up an amount or name"; `tests\test_cloud_fixes.py:383` `test_every_kind_from_sparse_input_has_no_stand_in_word`; for a new bot, default demo mode to **off** and print the mode in `/start` (`🌐 *Live Mode*` / `🧪 *Mock Mode*`).

### R2. Read every page, and prove you did

- **Rule:** follow the source's pager until the end, keep the query on every page, de-duplicate by the source's own id, cap the page count, and compare what you collected with the total the source states. A partial list is an error.
- **Incident:** `students.php` shows 50 students a page (330 on 7 pages on 28 Sep; 340 on 30 Sep). Every reader looked at page 1 only:
  - `/verified_date` saw 50 of 330 students, so **274 of 322** verifications were invisible: 12 Sep read **0** (truth 10), 14 Sep 4 (truth 9), 23 Jul **0** (truth 62).
  - The passport watcher saw **42 of 301** scans.
  - The phase-3 migration audit covered **78 of 326** students (page 1 per program, plus program names "KLP"/"EAP" that matched nobody and exited 0).
  - `/admitted` showed page 1 of all students as "admitted" (the portal ignores `?status=admitted`).
  - The old sheets builder had silently capped at 50 students (HANDOFF §3), which is why the sheets use the one-request CSV export.
- **Now:** `client.read_student_pages` / `read_students` (`client.py:454`, `:501`): pager `Page X of N · T students`, `?pg=N`, `MAX_STUDENT_PAGES = 40` (`:45`), `pages = max(pages, pager.pages)`, uid total check, de-duplication by uid (`e0d47ab`, `9dcd9ad`, `40da0e6`); `audit_program.py` follows the pager and exits 1 when a program matches nobody (`03ccf15`). The Supabase `student` kind is complete only from such a whole read; `/students` (page 1) publishes it partial.
- **Pin it:** `tests\test_foundation.py` "every page is read with its query and each student is listed once; a list that cannot be read whole raises"; `tests\test_jobs.py` "watcher reads every page (60 students over 2 pages → 60 audits)". The fake portal answers 404 for any page the test did not provide, so a reader that skips or invents a page fails.

### R3. Match dates on whole tokens, from the right element; never as substrings

- **Rule:** dates are parsed by one strict parser with whole-word month names and numeric boundaries, and only from the element that carries the date (the stamp's own element), never from the whole row's text.
- **Incident:**
  - Cross-checks matched the date as a substring of the whole student row. Every row holds a transfer-intake `<select>` with the option "2027 SEPTEMBER", which contains **"27 sep"**: 27 Sep returned **48 students instead of 2**, one 26,757-character message, and "Message is too long" (27 Sep 23:07). "Applied On" dates matched too, and a stray " E" from "Edit Info" leaked into stamp times.
  - Inquiries used the unanchored `0?{day}\s+{Mon}(?:\s+{year})?` with `re.search`: **"8 Sep" matched "18 Sep" and "28 Sep"** (34 counted vs 21 real).
  - A student name containing "mar", "jan" or "jun" was read as a month; free-text routing read "Janan" as January, "summary" as March, "doctor" as October, "daughter" as August, and routed "across" to cross-check and "shipping" to `/pin`.
- **Now:** `src\dates.py` `_USER_DATE_RE` (longest-first month alternation, `\b`, guards `(?<![\w/.:-])` / `(?![\w/.:-])`), `_STAMP_RE`, `parse_stamp`, `stamp_on_day` (`e0d47ab`); `parsers._student_details` reads the stamp from `.pf-by` only and `parsers.verification` picks students by that stamp (`40da0e6`); `ask.classify` builds every keyword with `_re(words) = re.compile(r"\b(?:" + words + r")\b", re.I)` (`7f42ad0`), including the performance words (`ask.py:103`, so "outperform" is not "performance").
- **Pin it:** `tests\test_crosscheck.py` "students are picked by their own stamp only (never the option '2027 SEPTEMBER' or 'Applied On')", "'Kumar' is a name, not a month"; `tests\test_inquiries.py` "8 Sep is never 18 or 28 Sep"; `tests\test_freetext.py` "across is no cross-check, shipping no /pin, Janan/summary/doctor/daughter are no months"; `tests\test_performance.py:763` `test_performance_words_are_whole_words`. Build synthetic rows with the decoy `<select>`s the live page has ([10 §2.1](10_TESTS_AND_VERIFICATION.md)).

### R4. Never fall back silently to today (or to any stand-in day)

- **Rule:** text that names no readable date, two dates, or an impossible date yields `None` plus a reason, and the reply says "I couldn't read that date (<why>)". Only an input that names no date at all *and* has no date-like leftovers may mean today, and only on lenient (free-text) paths. "Today" is always computed in the business time zone. The same holds for any period: a period you cannot serve is said so, never swapped for one you can.
- **Incident:** the baseline `normalize_date_input` returned today for anything it could not read, so `/inquiries_date 31 Sep` silently answered for the current day; a name misread as a month also became "today"; `parsers.normalize_target_date` fell back to the raw text; relative words used the PC clock (`datetime.now()`), not Asia/Dhaka. The yearless verification stamp ("27 Sep, 17:19") meant 27 Sep 2025 listed 2026's verifications. The first performance version answered "the month" with this month and "ytd performance" with today's page (review of 30 Sep).
- **Now:** `parse_user_date` / `user_date_problem` / `has_date_hint` (`src\dates.py`); `telegram_bot.normalize_date_input(text, strict)` returns `None` (`:88`); `replies.date_error_reply` ("Please send a date like `12 Sep 2026`…"); `_ask_date_again` keeps a pending "which date?" open for a mistyped answer (`:595`; `e164679`, `c17d887`); `yearless_day_problem` answers "not available" for a day a year or more back and for future days; `local_today()` uses `ZoneInfo(settings.REPORT_TIMEZONE)`; Jennie's `_words_date` refuses "31 September", "the 45th", `9월 31일` (`e164679`); `ask.performance_other_reply` (`ask.py:508`) for any period the performance commands do not cover; `records.performance_window` publishes nothing when the page's range cannot be read as two days.
- **Pin it:** `tests\test_foundation.py` (24 real forms read exactly; `31 Sep`, `29 Feb 2026`, `13/13/2026`, `Sep` → None, never today), `tests\test_repair_voice.py` (impossible spoken dates read nothing), `tests\test_performance.py:798`, `:821`. Freeze `local_today` in every test, in every module that holds its own name for it (R44).

### R5. A failed read is an error, never "0", "none" or an empty list

- **Rule:** readers raise a typed error with a plain reason; replies say "Couldn't read the portal: <why>. <what>: not available right now." Reserve 0 / "none" for pages that were read whole and show a real empty state. A copy published elsewhere follows the same rule: a failed read sends **nothing**, never an empty "complete" batch.
- **Incident:**
  - `get_verified_students` ignored a failed `login()` and turned every exception into `[]`, so the bot said "No student payments were verified" when it had read nothing.
  - On 28 Sep the consultation page changed layout; both readers required 8 cells, skipped all 500 rows, and the report said **"Total All-Time Done: 0"** and 0 received.
  - The calendar parser returned 11 reminders with every field empty after its layout change (the brief said "eleven deadlines") and 0 upcoming events.
  - Days older than about 3 weeks read 0 consultations (the list's 500-row cap, R18).
  - A timeout's error text is `''` (`str(httpx.ReadTimeout(''))`), which read as "no error, 0 reminders".
  - The 09:05 missing report could fail silently.
  - The performance review of 30 Sep: any one-cell table row counted as the page's empty state, which would have printed "nobody" for an error row.
- **Now:** `PortalUnavailable(reason, *, unreachable)` and `portal_error_reason` (`client.py:66-92`, `_error_text` never empty); `replies.portal_error_reply`; layout guards (R8); recognised empty states (`tr.empty-row` "No students found", `cr-empty-row` "No consultation requests yet.", "· 0 items", and only `tr.pf-empty-row > .pf-empty` on the performance page, `314afdb`); the brief's `not available (<why>)` per section; `missing_report.main` sends one plain failure message and exits 1 (`e164679`); in `src\cloud\` a read that failed becomes one `failed_reads` line and no batch ([13 §5](13_SUPABASE_PUBLISHING.md)).
- **Pin it:** for every reader, tests with the portal down, the login refused, the session expired and the layout unknown; each must produce the failure words (`tests\test_foundation.py`, `test_inquiries.py`, `test_brief.py`: "a failed read is never 'no payments'", "failures are said, never 0"; `tests\test_performance.py:351` `test_a_portal_error_row_is_never_nobody_is_listed`; `tests\test_cloud_commands.py:159`, `:211`, `:299`: "… that was not read publishes nothing").

### R6. Respect Telegram's 4096 limit and its Markdown parser

- **Rule:** send every reply that can grow through one splitter that counts UTF-16 code units, cuts between lines under ~3900, keeps formatting entities on one line, packs whole records per message, and resends a piece as plain text when Telegram refuses its Markdown.
- **Incident:** a 10,082-character `/verified` report and the 26,757-character cross-check (R3) failed with "Message is too long"; an LLM answer that was not valid legacy Markdown failed with "Can't parse entities" and the user got an error instead of the answer (27 Sep 20:19); code spans nested inside `_italics_` showed literal backticks in the date prompts; a long performance leaderboard could split one consultant's lines across two messages (review of 30 Sep).
- **Now:** `src\bot\replies.py` (`telegram_len`, `split_text`, `send_pieces`, `reply_long`, `markdown_to_plain`; `e0d47ab`, `e205327`); `telegram_bot._send_blocks` (`:1720`, whole student cards); `performance.message_pieces` (`performance.py:247`, whole consultant records); `scheduler._alert_messages` (`:104`, whole alert blocks); `brief.esc` / `_bold_safe` for escaping.
- **Pin it:** the fake `Message.reply_text` / `Sent.edit_text` raise `BadRequest("Message is too long")` over 4096 UTF-16 units and "Can't parse entities" when `refuse_markdown=True` ([10 §2.2](10_TESTS_AND_VERIFICATION.md)); assert the pieces rejoin to the text and no card is split (`tests\test_performance.py:586` `test_a_long_leaderboard_never_splits_a_consultants_record`).

### R7. The LLM never states a fact; check every sentence it writes

- **Rule:** code reads and counts. The LLM may only (a) pick indices of facts already computed (JSON schema output), (b) choose a command from an enum, or (c) word a short sentence that a deterministic checker verifies number by number, word by word and clause by clause; anything that fails is dropped, never repaired. Never give a model a template with slots the data does not fill. Measure the prompt before sending it. A downstream assistant that answers from your published data must follow the same rule: figures from rows, vectors only to find rows (D3).
- **Incident:**
  - The 18:05 "executive report" was written by the LLM from a four-section template that asked for visa counts, conversion rates, passport numbers, dates of birth and a scan-match verdict, none of which any reader provided. On 28 Sep ~11:50 it produced fake passport numbers (`1234567890…`), fake DOBs, "Scanned Image: Matched", a visas-YTD figure and a 28.4 % conversion rate. Its prompt was ~2,728 of 3,072 context tokens.
  - The free-text agent put the inquiry list into its prompt twice (~11k tokens by evening against `num_ctx` 3072); Ollama **silently kept only the last ~1,538 tokens**, dropping the system prompt with the data, and the model invented answers.
  - The 7B model once said a date in Korean as "27 사월 이천육 년" (27 April 2006); the small models miscount weekdays.
  - Jennie routed "passports" to a static text and said "5 verified today, 4 passports 100% match" when the truth was 0.
- **Now:** the factual brief in code (`src\bot\brief.py`, `f8fefa4`); `llm_summary` → `check_summary` → `claims_problem` (`brief.py:456`); `ollama_client.answer_agent_query` returns picked facts only, `_answer_query_fallback` first, `prompt_fits` (chars ÷ 2.4 ≤ `num_ctx` − 512) (`7f42ad0`, `d9bbecc`); Jennie: English checked by `_fact_problem` + `claims_problem`, Korean built in code, dates recomputed in code; no raw inquiry rows, contacts or remarks in any prompt; every Supabase record's text is built by code from fields that exist (`src\cloud\records.py`). Details: [06 §3-4](06_LLM_AND_JENNIE_VOICE.md).
- **Pin it:** `tests\test_brief.py` (`claims_problem` parametrised both ways: a number attached to the wrong fact is refused), `tests\test_freetext.py` ("the facts the LLM picks are shown word for word; it only picks indices; skips the LLM when the facts do not fit"), `tests\test_voice_fast.py` ("a number not in the answer is never spoken"). Accept that a strict checker drops some true summaries ([11 §B17](11_OPEN_ITEMS_AND_KNOWN_LIMITS.md)).

### R8. Parse by meaning, and guard the layout

- **Rule:** find columns by header names and cells by the page's own CSS classes; never by position. Before counting, prove the layout is the one you know: required columns exist, every row is recognised, counters are numeric, the page echoes the filter or period you asked for, and the page's own totals agree with the rows. An unknown layout raises; it never yields rows or zeros.
- **Incident:** within September 2026 the portal changed three pages on its own: `consult_requests.php` went from 8+ fixed columns to 7 named ones (all counts silently 0; `cd0277a`); `calendar.php` moved from `div.ag-item` to `.rm-item` / `.ev-row` / a `var EV` JSON list (empty reminders); `index.php` tile labels changed case (placeholders, R1). `students.php` positional parsing was one cell off. The CSV export's `Current Stage` / `Current Status` / `Progress %` were stale for 64-89 of 329 students. `consult_performance.php` falls back to This Month for any period it does not know, so a reader that does not check the echoed period can serve the wrong window.
- **Now:** `_STUDENT_COLUMNS` + `_REQUIRED_STUDENT_COLUMNS` (`parsers.py:321-324`) with `StudentListLayoutError` (unread rows raise); `_consult_columns` + tab/caption/filter-echo checks in `read_consultation_day` (rows == caption, rows ≤ All, per-status Counter == tabs); `parse_calendar_events(...)["layout_ok"]`; `parse_window_applications` needs a Status column; `ask._dashboard` raises when no fact can be read; `/stage` status and % from each `progress.php` (`e164679`); `parse_consult_performance` (`parsers.py:1088`, `_PERF_COLUMNS` `:963`, `_PERF_SHAPES` `:976`, `PerformanceLayoutError` `:955`) and the period echo check in `read_consult_performance` (`client.py:425-452`). Selector cheat sheet: [03b §4.11](03b_FILES_src_scraper_llm_api_config.md); page by page: [04 §3](04_PORTAL_INTEGRATION.md).
- **Pin it:** synthetic pages built from the live layout, including a "moved columns" and an "unknown layout" variant per page (`tests\test_consultations.py`: "an unknown layout gives no rows rather than wrong ones"; `tests\test_performance.py:373` `test_a_page_that_shows_another_period_is_refused`). After any source change, re-run the independent audit (Part B, M3). Study the live page shape with masked dumps (letters → `x`, digits → `9`).

### R9. Trust an OCR field only on proof

- **Rule:** an OCR reading counts as a confirmed discrepancy only when the document's own integrity check passes *and* the surrounding reading is sane; everything else is "check by eye". Keep two lists (confirmed vs uncertain). Try rotations before calling a scan unreadable, and never say "not a passport" when you merely could not read it.
- **Incident:**
  - The baseline read the MRZ only from the bottom 30 % of an upright image with a literal `P<`, so **4 of 7** flagged scans were real passports called "not a valid passport" (rotated PDFs and phone photos).
  - One-letter OCR misreads (N read as M) were reported as name discrepancies (token overlap 2/3 < 0.75 → TYPO); "100% Match across All Fields" was printed when Father/Mother/Address were not even on the scan.
  - **28 Sep 20:22, after the fixes:** a passport alert "Passport No mismatch" for one student: MRZ line 2 was garbled (nationality read "AZ7" instead of "BGD", the DOB and expiry check digits failed) but the passport-number check digit **passed by chance** (about 1 in 10), so `_validate_passport_data` trusted it (`ocr_validator.py:799`, `:809-812`). The printed page differed from the portal by one character: a question for staff, not a confirmed error.
- **Now:** rotations 0/270/90/180 (`ROTATIONS`), TD3 44-character validation, each MRZ field trusted only by its own check digit, `uncertain` list, `MRZ_UNREADABLE` wording, "All Fields" only when all 7 match (`40da0e6`). **Deferred** (owner: "not now"): trust `passport_no_ok` only when nationality equals line 1's state or ≥ 2 check digits agree; flag one-character printed-page differences; name the scan file and upload date in the alert ([11 §C](11_OPEN_ITEMS_AND_KNOWN_LIMITS.md)). The Supabase copy publishes no audit that checked nothing (`records.UNCHECKED_AUDITS`, `records.py:60`), so an unread scan never overwrites a real audit.
- **Pin it:** `tests\test_crosscheck.py` with a stub OCR engine and `mrz(...)` lines with real check digits; add the deferred case: a line 2 with a bad nationality and failing DOB/expiry digits but a passing document digit must give `NOT_READ`, not `MISMATCH`.

### R10. Mark an alert sent only after the recipient accepted it

- **Rule:** keep an outbox, not a "seen" set. Store each finding with `sent=false`, save after every unit of work (atomically), send, and flip `sent` only when the API accepted that message; stop at the first refusal and retry the rest next run. Key the memory by the identity of the thing checked (here the uploaded file), so a replacement is checked again. Never remember a result that was not actually checked. The same holds for any sink: a hash state advances only on an accepted call (R31).
- **Incident:** the old watcher cached every flagged uid and **saved before sending**, sent only `new_alerts[:3]`, and logged `len(new_alerts)`: **5 of 8** alerts on 27 Sep were never sent and were never retried (the owner declined to fix it in decision 9; it was fixed anyway in `9dcd9ad`). The cache was keyed by uid only, so a re-uploaded passport was never checked again. After the fix, a scan that returned `PORTAL_UNREADABLE` or `OCR_UNAVAILABLE` was still remembered as audited and never checked (`344a247`).
- **Now:** `data\alerted_passport_issues.json` v2 keyed `uid|passport_<uid>_<unix>.<ext>`; `save_watcher_cache` (`scheduler.py:63`, `.part` + `os.replace`) after every audit; `_send_alerts` (`:131`) marks `sent`/`sent_at` per accepted message; `UNCHECKED_STATUSES = ("MISSING_DOCUMENT", "PORTAL_UNREADABLE", "OCR_UNAVAILABLE")` are neither alerted nor remembered (`scheduler.py:39`); an old-format memory is distrusted and every scan is checked once more. First full run after deploy: 146 + 155 scans audited, 20 + 11 alerts in 3 messages. Only accepted alert messages become Supabase `notification`s (`bot_jobs.note_sent`).
- **Pin it:** `tests\test_jobs.py` "every alert is sent, grouped under the limit, and marked sent only after Telegram accepted it (a refused one goes on the next run)", "undownloadable scans are retried and never remembered".

### R11. Key records by a stable identity; placeholders are no identity

- **Rule:** choose the row key by preference (assigned id → a real document number → name + phone), treat shared placeholders (`PENDING`, blanks) as no identity, and when a key changes pair old and new rows (surest match first, each once) and report an *edit*, not "one left, one joined". Two different birth dates are never one person. Prefer the source's own hidden id when it has one.
- **Incident:** the sync keyed rows by Student ID, else Passport No, else name + mobile. A Student ID is assigned at payment verification, so **every verification showed as "1 new + 1 removed"**. Many students share the placeholder passport "PENDING"; the issue-date cache keyed on it gave **12 students another student's date**. After the pairing fix, a sibling leaving and another joining with the same family mobile merged into a false edit (re-verify, fixed in `c17d887`). `doc_verifier.portal_students()` keys by passport, so one student whose portal record carries the head of branch's passport number is hidden (open). The first Supabase build keyed consultations by `sha1(name + contact + received)`, which changes on every edit (review of 29 Sep).
- **Now:** `auto_sync._row_key`, `_passport_no` (≥ 6 characters with a digit), `_same_student` (3/2/1), `_likely_same` (mobile + a like name, or DOB + name; different DOBs → 0), duplicate keys get `#2`, `#3` (`9dcd9ad`, `e164679`, `c17d887`); `passport_issue.passport_key`; the consultation record key is the hidden `id` of the row's forms (`3caa393`; all 1,034 on 30 Sep); `student_index` gives a passport shared by two students to neither.
- **Pin it:** `tests\test_jobs.py` "a student given an ID is 'edited (Student ID)', not new + removed; PENDING is no identity"; `tests\test_final_fixes.py` "a sibling with the same mobile and another DOB is not an edit"; `tests\test_cloud_fixes.py:500` `test_a_consultation_is_keyed_by_the_hidden_id_of_its_forms`.

### R12. Budget the GPU on paper; isolate heavy GPU work in fresh processes

- **Rule:** list every consumer of the card with its measured peak, add the desktop, and keep the sum under the card with margin; one small model with one fixed context size; load it on demand unless latency needs it resident; run batch OCR in fresh processes of a few items; never trust a free-memory probe alone; put anything that does not need the GPU (embeddings) on the CPU and **prove** the GPU is hidden.
- **Incident (8 GB RTX 5060):**
  - The monitor is plugged into the RTX, so Windows and apps hold **~1.2-1.4 GB**.
  - With voice on, brain 2.6-2.8 + TTS 3.4 + STT 2.2 + desktop ≈ **8-9.8 GB** peaks against 7.96 GB usable. Windows (WDDM system-memory fallback) does not fail; it **spills to shared RAM** and everything slows: Korean TTS 2-7×, a 25.8 s voice note instead of ~12 s.
  - `torch.cuda.mem_get_info` did not see the resident brain, so the service's 3.8 GB gate moved TTS onto a full card.
  - `qwen2.5:7b` (4.4-4.7 GB) left ~2.2 GB and pushed Whisper and CosyVoice to the CPU (a 4 s Korean reply took 64 s).
  - One long document-OCR process grew to 7 GB VRAM + 6 GB spilled and slowed from ~90 to ~570 s per student; `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=garbage_collection_threshold` made it worse.
  - On the old PC a second OCR process hit `CUDA out of memory — 7.93 GiB`.

### R30. A scope must not change while its window grows

- **Rule:** a delete scope must name what a complete read covers **in a form that stays the same for as long as that read keeps covering it**. A window that grows day by day (this month, this week, "to date") is scoped by its **first** day only; the moving last day goes into the record's data and `day`. Otherwise each day's read opens a new scope, and a key that drops out stays forever in yesterday's scope, which no read covers again.
- **Incident:** the release preflight (30 Sep 21:22, task `w07j1ddbg`) found the new kind `consultant_performance` scoped `month|<first>|<last>`. The portal ends every period at today ("01 Oct – 02 Oct", then "01 Oct – 03 Oct"), so from 1 October the scope would change daily, and a consultant who dropped off the leaderboard would never be deleted (against D10). Fixed before publishing went on.
- **Now:** `records.performance_scope` (`records.py:823-830`) returns `"<period>|<first ISO day>"` (`month|2026-09-01`, `today|2026-09-30`); `day` = the range's last day; `data.range`, `first_day`, `last_day` as printed (`8317741`). Past windows (each day's `today`, each past month) are never deleted by a later window: they stay as history, and Jeannie must date them. The module docstring `command_hooks.py:45-49` still shows the old form (stale, [13 §18](13_SUPABASE_PUBLISHING.md)).
- **Pin it:** `tests\test_cloud_performance.py:333` `test_the_month_scope_stays_the_same_as_the_portals_range_grows`; for every new kind, a test that reads the same window on two consecutive days and asserts one scope and exactly the dropped key deleted.

### R31. Delete only what a provably complete read no longer shows

- **Rule:** a downstream copy deletes a key only when **this** read was provably whole (every page, the source's own total or count badge matched, no error, no timeout, every row became a record) and **newer** than the last complete read of that scope. Send the complete key list only on the last call, forget a deleted key locally only **after** the store accepted the delete, never let a stale "nothing changed" shortcut survive an unanswered call, and never send an empty complete list from a failed read. When in doubt, partial. One writer at a time.

### R32. With hash-gated upserts, everything outside the hash must follow from it

- **Rule:** when the store skips a row whose content hash is unchanged, it writes **nothing** for that row: not the read time, not the run, not the denormalised columns, not the vectors. So (a) derive every column you want kept current from the hashed fields (put the ids into `data`), (b) never change a hash just to refresh a timestamp (freshness comes from the run log), and (c) guard against changing anything the hash does not cover (the embedding model) with a local check that refuses to publish.
- **Incident:** the contract review (29 Sep) found `student_uid` null on the passport-keyed kinds (the document check knows a student by passport only) — and a uid learned later would never reach a row whose hash did not change. The SQL keeps a same-hash row's old `read_at` and `run_id` (declined by the fixer as a SQL matter: `read_at` means "last changed"). A model change would leave unchanged rows with the old model's vectors, a mix Jeannie cannot compare against. `blank_on_portal` was added only when non-empty so that 13,000 records without a filler kept their hash.

### R33. Cap every request body by its exact serialised size

- **Rule:** a row cap is not a size cap. Split calls by the **exact bytes you will send** (the empty body, each row's JSON, the separators, and the key list on the last call), under a budget well below any gateway limit you have not measured; send an over-sized single row alone with one line naming its size (never its content).
- **Incident:** dry run 1 (29 Sep): the largest `hg_sync` body was **1.98 MB** (200 student rows with every detail field and embeddings); the Supabase gateway's real limit was unknown and still is.

- **Pin it:** `tests\test_cloud_dry_run.py:310` `test_calls_are_split_to_stay_under_the_body_cap_with_the_key_list_on_the_last`, `:331`, `:347`, `:364`, `:384`.

### R34. Hide the GPU with a non-empty value, before torch loads, and prove it

- **Rule:** a process that must not touch the GPU sets `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=-1` (never `""`, W13) at the very top of its entry point, before anything can import torch; refuses to embed if torch was already imported with CUDA visible; and a test starts a **real** child the way production does and reads the OS-level variable. Only such a process may run the model.
- **Incident:** dry run 1 (29 Sep 14:39): `embed.prepare_process` set `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=''`; on Windows the variable vanished from the real environment, and `torch.cuda.is_available()` was True in the embedding process. The model stayed on the CPU only because torch still refused the device.
- **Now:** `embed.NO_GPU = "-1"` (`embed.py:43`), `prepare_process()` (`:60-73`, raises `EmbedError` if torch is already imported with CUDA visible; also sets `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1`, quiet loggers), `cpu_only_process()` (`:87`, accepts only "-1"); `if __name__ == "__main__": os.environ["CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES"] = "-1"` at the top of `publish.py` (`:60-61`), `backfill.py` and `full_picture.py`; `handoff.child_env()` (`handoff.py:93`); `publish._can_embed_here` hands over instead of embedding in any other process (the bot process has torch with CUDA for the passport OCR) (`752cd53`). - **Pin it:** `tests\test_cloud_dry_run.py:259` `test_the_publisher_process_started_as_the_handoff_starts_it_sees_no_gpu`, `:269` `test_every_entry_point_hides_the_gpu_with_minus_one`, `:277` `test_an_empty_cuda_value_is_not_a_cpu_only_process`.

### R37. Know which key is which: personal access token, secret key, publishable key

- **Rule:** a hosted database has several credentials with very different reach. Name each one in `.env.example`, use the **least** that works per process, never put an account-wide token where a project key will do, load a CLI token into that one process's environment (never a command line or a chat), and never write a typed setting empty.

### R38. Never stop, restart or kill a process you did not start

### R39. After a network drop, resume the saved run; a step that ran without the network proved nothing

- **Pin it:** make a reviewer's "no issues" require evidence (files read, harnesses run, mutations killed); treat an agent error as a failed step, never a pass.

## Part B. The development method that worked

### M1. The grill-me interview (before touching anything)

1. **Audit the docs first, read-only.** Workflow `wd9k5egdv` (9 agents, one per dimension: paths, bootstrap, cold start, environment, runtime, integrity, outbound side effects) checked every claim of MIGRATION.md, PC_BUILD.md and HANDOFF.md against the code and the new PC: **126 findings**, the 47 blocker/high ones re-checked adversarially. Examples: `E:` hard-coded in 11 files (the doc listed 5), `rich` and `pypdf` missing from the install line, the CPU-only torch trap, the auto-claim hole, mock mode by default.
2. **Then interview the owner one question at a time**, each with the options, the consequences and a recommended option marked; the owner chooses. 18 migration decisions on 27 Sep (e.g. 1 "this is the new PC", 2 "old PC gone", 5 "configurable paths under `C:\Hangeul`", 9 "close only the auto-claim hole", 12 "phase by phase, checked", 13 "local git, baseline first"), J1-J15 for the voice assistant, and on 29 Sep four questions before the Supabase build (the public Jeannie app is an accepted risk; the project `dcbcbpwpmdtaanboetiz`; yes to the model download; wait for the preconditions). The owner overruled the recommendation several times (no rotation, keep apostille-first as FAIL, leave Konyang in Drive, wait for the preconditions), and those answers stand.
3. **Write every decision down** with "why" and "how to apply" in a persistent note (`memory\hangeul-migration-decisions.md`, `hangeul-jarvis-plan.md`), including "don't re-propose unless asked" for declined items (decisions 9 and 18, the public Jeannie app, key rotation). A spec from the owner with its own decisions (D1-D13 in `hangeul-bot-prompt.md`) is followed as written: "do not re-open them". Full list: [08 §3, §10](08_HISTORY_STAGE_BY_STAGE.md).

### M2. Phase-by-phase migration

### M6. Deploy only after a scheduled job has run, then watch the next runs

### M7. Working rules for the agent

- Never type or echo a secret; write the owner's pasted values into `.env` without repeating them; warn when a secret has been pasted into chat; name secrets as "value in secrets/bot.env (KEY)"; load a CLI token into one process's environment only.
- Ask before downloads, installs and any action with an outside effect; the owner approved each (Ollama installer, models, trials, the embedding model, the Supabase CLI, the migrations, the backfill, switching publishing on).
- Never open student images; replace student data with placeholders in tests, fixtures, commit messages and reports; delete payload folders only with the owner's OK ([11 §F](11_OPEN_ITEMS_AND_KNOWN_LIMITS.md)).
- Never stop, restart or kill a process you did not start (R38); verify in worktrees, never in `C:\Hangeul\BOT`; running pytest in the live folder writes caches, so run it in a clean `git archive` copy ([10 §1](10_TESTS_AND_VERIFICATION.md)).
- Long jobs detached, checked by process; code written with an editor tool (W3, W10).

### M8. The guarded release of a data pipeline (29-30 Sep)

1. **Guarded dry runs** of the real backfill entry point in a worktree with byte-identical copies of the git-ignored inputs, waiting for the live sync to release its lock (a worktree run cannot see it); a network guard at the HTTP, DNS and connect layers; the payloads checked by an independent verifier with its own session, parsers, decoder and scanners (checks a-l, [13 §15.4](13_SUPABASE_PUBLISHING.md)). Three runs on 29 Sep: not ready, not ready, **ready**.
2. **The owner's go**, after a report with one masked example per kind; the owner chose to inspect the payloads first.
3. **The release merge** as a real `git merge --no-ff main` in a worktree, looking for meaning conflicts that produce no markers (a renamed helper, two date pins, a wrapped-soup count), then a **preflight** of six checks: fast-forwardable, the full suite, nothing lost from either parent, a fourth dry run, live commands with fake Telegram, and "off publishes nothing". Its low finding (R30) was fixed before going on.
4. **Deploy with the switch off**, then the **backfill** with the switch on in that one process, then an **independent postcheck** of the store itself (GETs only), then **switch on** at a quiet moment and watch the first runs ([10 §4.11-§4.12](10_TESTS_AND_VERIFICATION.md)).

### M9. Resume, don't restart, an interrupted workflow

- Keep each step's result on disk (the workflow's own saved state and a JSON result file per task), so an agent or network failure is resumed from the last good step with the finished agents cached (29 Sep 13:23; 30 Sep 10:10).
- Before resuming: check the network, the branch state (clean, no half-written commit) and the live system (untouched). Treat any step that errored as not run, whatever its summary says (R39).

## Part D. Adapting to another agency

Hangeul BOT hard-codes a lot of Hangeul, its portal and Bangladesh: page paths and column words, the program and stage names, the dashboard tile labels, the performance page's columns and tiles, the manager's name in the e-mail prompt (`telegram_bot.py:1318-1320`, `:1350`), the Drive folder ids, the phone format (`880`), the common Bangladeshi name words, the passport-number shapes, the taka thresholds, the July–June fiscal year, the NID lengths, the apostille hosts, the HSC/SSC level names, the job times and quiet windows, and, in the publish layer, the Supabase project, the 26 kinds and their English text templates, the filler words and status fields, and the embedding model. The full list, with `path:line`, the Hangeul value and what the new agency supplies, is **[12 Adaptation map](12_ADAPTATION_MAP.md)** (the Supabase constants in its §8); it ends with the parts that are generic and can be copied as they are (`replies.py`, `dates.py`, `claims_problem`, `PortalUnavailable`/`portal_get`, the all-pages reader, the watcher outbox, `_run_module`, the locks, `net_fix.py`, `redact()`, `decode_cf_emails`, the publish engine, the handoff and the embedder).

Order of work for a new agency: (1) survey the new portal with [04 §8](04_PORTAL_INTEGRATION.md) (including whether a CDN rewrites its pages) and write its synthetic fixtures; (2) put every item of [12 §1-§8](12_ADAPTATION_MAP.md) into settings (`.env` or one agency module) instead of literals; (3) set up Google from zero ([02 §3.5](02_ENVIRONMENT_SETUP_AND_OPERATIONS.md)) and, if a second store is wanted, the database from the reader's repo ([02 §3.11](02_ENVIRONMENT_SETUP_AND_OPERATIONS.md)); (4) follow Part C.
